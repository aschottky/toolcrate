# Audience (HeyCatch, Aug 2026)

Source: HeyCatch Foundation > Audience. Quotes are from competitor and category sites HeyCatch crawled, not from ToolCrate customers.

## Summary
- Who has this problem? Local service-business owners (contractors, heavy trades, specialized professional firms) whose websites "look fine but don't convert visitors into calls" (sunshineweb.design).
- Who pays for this problem? The subset already spending money on a solution: a $500-$2,500 freelancer build, a $2,500-$15,000 agency site, or a DIY Wix/Squarespace subscription that isn't generating leads (rasmussenresults.com).
- Dominant pain: "Most local service sites are brochures. They look okay but don't convert visitors into calls" (ecommerceparadise.com).
- Dominant objection: the build is priced at $497 for "high-stakes" professional work, a suspiciously low number that reads as "probably a template" to buyers told "anyone who quotes you a single number before asking a single question is guessing" (grindstonedesign.com). Inference, since the site has zero testimonials, logos, or named cases to counter it.
- Dominant trigger: "Three months after [launch], the phone isn't ringing. The owner wonders if the website was worth it" (outboundautonomy.com).

## ICP 1: Residential trade contractor owner-operator
- Who: solo or small-crew owner of a roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, or excavation business, roughly $200K-$2M revenue.
- Desired result: "More calls, more requests, more booked work" without becoming a marketer.
- Functional job: when my website sits there looking fine but the phone isn't ringing, I want a site engineered to drive calls and quote requests, so I can book more jobs.
- Emotional job: feel that a professional has it handled; "you shouldn't also have to be a marketer."
- Pains (their words): "Most local service sites are brochures"; "Template sites sold as custom. Most agencies reuse the same layout across industries"; "70% of traffic comes from phones" and a missing mobile call button loses leads.
- Objections:
  - "Is this just a template with my logo swapped in?" The $497 price makes this the top fear the site must disarm.
  - "No retainer traps / surprise rebuild." Competitors sell "no monthly retainer traps"; the $79/mo needs justification.
  - "Do I actually own it?" "100% you own the site" is a headline competitor promise; ToolCrate's FAQ answers it ("no hostage situations") but it should be one click from the hero.
  - "Who's actually behind this?" No client logos, testimonials, company name, or address; a trust gap for a trade owner used to referrals.
- Triggers: a paid site that stopped producing ("three months after that, the phone isn't ringing"); a competitor who hired someone who knows what they're doing.
- Current solutions: local freelancer ($500-$2,500), template agency ($2,500-$5,000), custom agency ($5,000-$15,000), flat-rate contractor packages ($1,000-$2,000). ToolCrate's $497 undercuts all of them.
- Vocabulary: insiders say "make the phone ring," "booked jobs," "click-to-call," "the trades," "service and city pages." Outsider tells: "digital ghost town," "get my free roast," "pretty for pretty's sake."
- Where they are: r/Construction, r/HVAC, r/roofing, r/Plumbing, r/electricians, r/Contractor; field/CRM tools and Google Business Profile. Buying path: peer recommendation plus "contractor website cost" searches landing on flat-rate package pages.

## ICP 2: Specialized local professional firm
- Who: 1-10-person family-law, medical/dental, or consulting practice where local reputation drives revenue.
- Desired result: a site that makes the practice "feel established before the first quote request lands."
- Functional job: when a prospect checks us out before calling, I want a site that signals credibility instantly, so I win the engagement.
- Emotional job: confidence the site matches the quality of the practice, not "outdated and does not match the quality of my work."
- Pains: "People need to trust us before they email or request a quote"; "My website looks outdated and does not match the quality of my work"; "Trust signals and social proof drive conversion more than design."
- Objections: "$497 feels too cheap for high-stakes work"; "Where is the proof / who are your clients?"; "Will I be locked in / can I leave?" (ownership answer is buried below the fold).
- Triggers: losing an engagement to a better-looking competitor; a new partner pushing to modernize before a marketing push.
- Current solutions: agency lead-gen builds ($5,000-$15,000 for a 10-25 page site) or an outdated legacy site.
- Vocabulary: "every lead is worth real money," "local reputation," "trust signals," "booked appointments." They avoid trade-shop slang like "roast" and "heavy trades."
- Where they are: r/smallbusiness, r/Lawyertalk, r/Dentistry, r/consulting; buy via referral and category search, validated by visible proof.

## ICP 3: DIY-fatigued small service owner on Wix/Squarespace
- Who: owner who self-built on a DIY builder and is frustrated by the time sink and lack of leads. ToolCrate's problem card names it: "Every hour you spend tweaking Wix is an hour you're not making money."
- Desired result: stop babysitting the site and get "leads without lifting a finger."
- Functional job: when I've spent months tweaking my DIY site with nothing to show, I want a pro to build it right, so I stop wasting billable hours.
- Emotional job: relief that it's off my plate and being done properly.
- Pains: "DIY builders like Wix are tempting but they quietly kill your Google rankings through slow load times and poor technical SEO"; "You're great at your trade. You shouldn't also have to be a marketer"; "I do not have time to manage updates, backups, monitoring, hosting, and technical issues."
- Objections: "Will a redesign actually get me leads, or is it money down the drain?" (free preview is the answer); "Do I need an engineer to run it after handoff?" ($79/mo care plan; explain it near the CTA); "Can I get my money back if I don't like it?" (30-day guarantee exists but isn't surfaced on the hero).
- Triggers: DIY hours pile up with no calls; a slow/low-ranking site becomes obviously uncompetitive.
- Current solutions: Wix/Squarespace/GoDaddy at $0-$500/yr plus unpaid labor. Managed alternatives start "as low as $99/month."
- Vocabulary: "tweaking Wix," "done-for-you," "free mockup," "without lifting a finger," "loads slow." They avoid agency jargon like "information architecture."
- Where they are: r/smallbusiness, r/Wix, r/squarespace, r/Entrepreneur, r/marketing; buy via search ("best website for small business," "Wix alternative") and the pull of a free demo.

## Anti-patterns (what buyers complain about; each is a positioning opening)
- "Template sold as custom." ToolCrate's "no recycled templates" counters it, but at $497 with no proof, buyers may assume the opposite.
- Retainer traps and surprise rebuilds. ToolCrate's flat $79/mo care plan fits, if clearly scoped.
- Hostage code / no ownership. "No hostage situations" directly answers it.
- "It depends" pricing runaround. Transparent $497 + $79/mo is a differentiator against opaque agency quotes.
- No proof / no accountability. Competitors lead with results ("+383% avg booking lift," "4.9 stars across 80+ clients"). ToolCrate's zero testimonials/logos is the biggest gap versus the field.
